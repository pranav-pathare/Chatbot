# Campaign Lifecycle

## Overview

The campaign lifecycle is the end-to-end process of taking an advertising campaign
from an initial brief through to a final performance analysis. Although tools and
teams vary, most digital campaigns move through the same stages: planning, setup and
trafficking, launch and QA, optimization, reporting, and wrap-up. Treating these as
distinct phases keeps work organized and makes it clear who owns each handoff.

## 1. Planning and Strategy

Everything starts with a **brief** that states the objective, audience, budget,
timing, and success metrics (KPIs). From the brief, a **media plan** is built:
channels and placements are selected, budgets are allocated, and forecasts estimate
reach and results. The plan is formalized in an **insertion order (IO)** that defines
what will run, where, when, and at what cost. Clear KPIs defined here — such as CPA,
CTR, viewability, or ROAS — are what every later stage is measured against.

## 2. Setup and Trafficking

In this phase the media plan is implemented in the ad platforms. Campaigns,
placements, and line items are created in tools like Campaign Manager 360, Display &
Video 360, or Google Ad Manager; creatives are uploaded and assigned; targeting,
pacing, and frequency caps are configured; and tracking tags (including Floodlight or
analytics tags) are generated and shared with partners for implementation.
Consistency between the plan and the trafficked setup is essential for clean
reporting later.

## 3. Launch and QA

Before and immediately after go-live, **quality assurance** verifies that everything
works: creatives render correctly across placements, click-through URLs and landing
pages resolve, tracking fires, targeting is applied as intended, and budgets and
flight dates are correct. Early monitoring confirms that impressions are actually
delivering and pacing as expected, so problems are caught in the first hours rather
than after budget is wasted.

## 4. Optimization

Once a campaign is live and gathering data, it is actively managed against its KPIs.
Common levers include reallocating budget toward better-performing placements or
audiences, pausing underperformers, adjusting bids or bid strategies, refreshing
creatives to combat fatigue, and tightening or broadening targeting. Pacing is watched
so the campaign neither underspends nor exhausts its budget too early. Optimization is
iterative and continues throughout the flight.

## 5. Reporting and Analysis

Throughout and after the flight, performance is reported against the KPIs set in
planning. Interim reports support optimization decisions; the **post-campaign
analysis** (often a wrap report) summarizes results, explains what drove them, and
captures learnings. Data is typically pulled from the ad servers and analytics tools,
reconciled across sources, and presented with context rather than raw numbers alone.

## 6. Wrap-Up and Learnings

The final stage closes the loop: billing is reconciled against delivered impressions,
discrepancies between platforms are resolved, and insights are documented so the next
brief starts smarter. Strong teams treat the wrap-up not as paperwork but as the
input to the next planning cycle, turning each campaign into a source of repeatable
improvement.
